Model & method

How this prediction works

This tool was built as part of a short-term internship under the Intel® Unnati — AI for Medical Intelligence program, structured as a supervised machine-learning classification task on a public healthcare stroke dataset (5,110 patient records).

01 · Data preparation

Missing BMI values were imputed with the dataset median, categorical fields (gender, work type, residence, marital status, smoking status) were one-hot encoded, and numerical fields (age, glucose level, BMI) were standardized with a StandardScaler before training.

02 · Model comparison

Logistic Regression and Random Forest classifiers were trained with class_weight="balanced" to counter the dataset's class imbalance — stroke-positive cases are a small minority of records.

03 · Evaluation focus

Accuracy alone is misleading on imbalanced medical data. Models were compared on precision, recall, F1-score, ROC–AUC and confusion matrix, with recall weighted most heavily since missing a genuine high-risk patient carries a higher cost than a false alarm.

04 · Deployed model

The {{ model_name }} classifier was selected for deployment based on its recall performance on held-out test data, and is served here through a Flask backend.

Limitations

The training data is a single public dataset with known class imbalance and self-reported lifestyle fields. Predictions reflect statistical correlation within that dataset, not clinical causation, and should never substitute a physician's evaluation.
